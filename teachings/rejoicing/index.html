This Week’s Teaching

The reset is simpler than we think. When we lose our way, we don’t need a new personality — we need to hear the Father again. Skip the little check-ins and the rejoicing thins out: complaints get loud, hope gets deferred, the heart grows sick, and even the body starts to feel it. But Psalm 65 says God’s paths still drop fatness — even on the pastures of the wilderness.

The Three Steps
  1. 1. Hear the Father — Proverbs 13:1 · Deuteronomy 6:6–7 · Matthew 4:4 A wise son hears his father’s instruction. Stay close enough to catch it — morning, noon, and night. You can be stuffed with bread and still be empty.
  2. 2. Name the Drain — Proverbs 13:12 · 14:30 · 17:22 · Romans 12:2 Unplugged, unfed, mixed-up. Hope deferred makes the heart sick; a broken spirit dries the bones. The Word is the regulator, and a renewed mind is the fix.
  3. 3. Let It Drop — Psalm 65:11–13 Verse 11 is the source: God crowns the year. Verse 12 is the landing: the wilderness, not the already-pretty garden. Verse 13 is the result: the land shouts for joy and sings.
Psalm 65:12, Side by Side
  1. KJV “They drop upon the pastures of the wilderness: and the little hills rejoice on every side.”
  2. NKJV “They drop on the pastures of the wilderness, And the little hills rejoice on every side.”
  3. ESV “The pastures of the wilderness overflow, the hills gird themselves with joy.”
  4. NIV “The grasslands of the wilderness overflow; the hills are clothed with gladness.”
Your Move
  1. 1. Read Proverbs 13 slowly today.
  2. 2. Read Psalm 65:11–13 in KJV, NKJV, ESV, and NIV.
  3. 3. Ask: Father, where have I stopped hearing You?
  4. 4. Name one wilderness pasture — and let His goodness drop on it.
This Week’s Challenge

Set three check-ins with the Father this week — morning, noon, and night. Keep them short. Keep them daily. Then watch what happens to the dry places.
